# Where it comes from, and why

What is documented so far, kept separate from what is still a question. This file is thin on purpose: it only holds what a checked source supports.

## What is documented

### Domestic events drive it, and it concentrates on Twitter

Where it comes from is hard to tell. Accounts can claim any country, and influence operations use accounts that pass as local, so a post's apparent origin is weak evidence. The sources below show what drives spikes, not who posts.


- **CCDH and the Human Rights Campaign (2022):** nearly a million tweets in seven months used slurs such as "groomer" about LGBTQ+ people, and "grooming" talk spiked after a specific law was passed. Political events drive the rhetoric.
- **CCDH, "The Musk Bump" (2022):** tweets using one transphobic slur rose 62% (3,159 to 5,117 a day) after Twitter changed owner. Platform rules and enforcement change how much of it appears.
- **ISD (2025):** over 97,000 anti-LGBTQ+ posts from more than 1,000 violent extremist channels between August 2024 and January 2025. Targeted hate doubled around the US election, and anti-trans content rose to 46% of it. Elections and offline events drive spikes, and extremist networks are a source.
- **Beth's Anti-Transphobia Library, ["Twitter transphobe stats"](https://bethylamine.github.io/library/whatisawomanbot/twitter_transphobe_stats)** (undated; checked): a summary of 52,401 Twitter accounts marked as transphobic through the [Soupcan](https://bethylamine.github.io/library/browser-extension/#soupcan) browser extension. Of the 22,159 accounts whose bio location could be placed on a map, 38% were in the US and 34% in the UK. Weighted by how many Twitter users each country has (Oberlo's figures), the author puts UK accounts at 3.8 times as likely as US accounts to be on the list. Most were small: 72% followed more accounts than followed them, and about 60% of their tweets came from accounts with under 1,000 followers. What it cannot show: the marks come from user reports, moderators and automated labels, which the extension's own page warns can be wrong; location is what people wrote in their bios; the page gives no date; and following more accounts than follow you is not evidence of bots. It fits the open question below on the British gender-critical movement, but one crowdsourced list does not settle it.
- **Our measurements (October 2026):** the slur "troon" is used about 2,550 times a day on Twitter in original English posts, and about 10 times a day on Bluesky. The same rhetoric is roughly 250 times as common on Twitter. On Bluesky, most posts using anti-trans terms were pushing back against them.

### It recycles older homophobia

- **Beth's Anti-Transphobia Library, "Moral Panic: Recycled Homophobia"** traces current anti-trans talking points to earlier campaigns against gay people (checked). The "groomer" slur is one example.

### Foreign influence operations use it as a wedge

- **DFRLab (2024):** the Russian operation Doppelganger ran a fake LGBTQ+ outlet with topics including "Trans youth" and "Woke Wars", alongside narratives against Ukraine aid (checked). Anti-trans topics are used among other culture-war wedges, not as the main goal.
- **EUvsDisinfo (2025):** pro-Kremlin messaging in Moldova cast "an LGBTIQ+ agenda" and the undermining of "traditional values" as Western impositions (noted in the Bot Traffic Monitor's research; recheck on the original page before citing).
- **What is not shown:** no source we checked measures what share of anti-trans rhetoric online is foreign. Claims that it is "mostly bots" or "mostly Russian" are not supported by anything here.

### Individual claims start with identifiable people

- The "wound" myth is reported to trace to a book by Germaine Greer (*unverified*).
- The "no such thing as trans kids" claim is documented from a New York City education official in 2023; we have no source on where the phrase originated.

## Open questions

These need research, each answered from checked sources:

1. **Organised networks.** Which organisations write and fund anti-trans legislation and messaging (for example in the US, UK and EU), and what do investigative reports and court records show about their funding?
2. **Religious and "traditional values" movements.** How do transnational religious-right networks spread the rhetoric between countries?
3. **The gender-critical movement.** Where did the UK gender-critical movement come from, and how did its arguments move into US right-wing politics?
4. **Media.** How much coverage do major outlets give to anti-trans framing, and has that been measured (for example by GLAAD or Trans Journalists Association media studies)?
5. **Platforms.** What do platform transparency reports and researchers say about enforcement of hate-speech rules on Twitter, Meta and Bluesky since 2022?
6. **Foreign share.** Has any study measured the share of anti-trans rhetoric coming from state influence operations, as opposed to domestic accounts?
7. **Harm.** What does research show about links between online anti-trans rhetoric and offline harassment, violence, or mental-health outcomes for trans people?
8. **Each talking point's origin.** For each entry in [Talking points](talking-points.md), the earliest documented use and who popularised it.
